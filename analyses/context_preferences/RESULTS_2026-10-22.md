# Initial results: 17-area analysis

These results use public datacube cache `v0.0.289`, 1,999 permutations, and the
definitions in the [analysis guide](README.md). The dataset includes 35,170
neurons from 81 good-behavior expert sessions (34 mice), plus 406 neurons from
one anatomically annotated naive session. All 82 sessions completed their
statistical analyses without errors.

The primary results below use the **0–100 ms** response window. Estimates weight
sessions equally within each mouse, then mice equally. Reported q-values are BH
corrected across the complete area-summary family. Permutation significance is
conditional on the recorded sessions; the mouse bootstrap intervals separately
describe uncertainty across animals. These analyses include all QC-passing cells,
without requiring significant baseline context modulation or a successful GLM fit.

## 1. Modality preference beyond chance

All 17 expert areas show positive cross-validated modality power and positive
target-to-nontarget modality generalization, with q = 0.00184 and positive mouse
bootstrap intervals for both statistics. The mouse-weighted fraction of cells
with nominal modality p ≤ .05 ranges from 11.9% in CA1 to 66.9% in MRN, above the
joint stimulus-shuffle expectation in every area. These are nominal selective
fractions, not fractions passing cell-level FDR correction.

The generalization result supports modality-related response organization that
extends across the two tested identities within each modality. It does not by
itself establish distinct anatomical pathways or generalization to untested stimuli.

## 2. Baseline context preference and modality preference

Positive cross-half associations pass q < .05 in ACAd, CP, MOp, MOs, MRN, ORBl,
ORBvl, SCm, SSp, and VISp. Each also has a positive mouse bootstrap interval.
Restricting the cell-pairing permutation to cells on the same probe retains
significance in these same ten areas.

For the five explicitly added areas:

| Area | QC neurons | Mice supporting this statistic | Cross-half Spearman correlation | Mouse bootstrap 95% interval | q |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| MRN | 599 | 12 | 0.256 | [0.157, 0.360] | 0.00249 |
| SCm | 905 | 17 | 0.317 | [0.194, 0.430] | 0.00249 |
| ACAd | 973 | 14 | 0.128 | [0.029, 0.228] | 0.00905 |
| ACAv | 965 | 10 | 0.071 | [−0.014, 0.151] | 0.0545 |
| ORBvl | 956 | 8 | 0.109 | [0.005, 0.227] | 0.0152 |

SCm is exactly SCiw + SCig + SCdg. The number of mice supporting a statistic can
be smaller than the anatomical coverage because a session needs at least ten
cells in that area for population association statistics. Lack of significance
in the remaining areas does not establish the absence of an association.

## 3. Baseline context preference and target preference

The association with target preference pooled across modalities is significant
only in CP (correlation 0.059, mouse interval [0.018, 0.099], q = 0.0112).
This pooled contrast can miss a relationship whose direction differs between
visual-preferring and auditory-preferring cells.

The complementary test asks whether target-minus-nontarget preference is larger
in the modality matching a cell's independently estimated baseline context
preference. SCm is the only area passing q < .05 for this early-window test:
normalized advantage 0.0459, mouse interval [0.0258, 0.0662], q = 0.00690.
This is an association across cells, not evidence that baseline activity causes
the target response. The full tables also contain separate visual and auditory
target contrasts and late-window sensitivity analyses.

## 4. Naive comparison

Only one naive mouse has QC-passing neurons with anatomical assignments in this
public cache. Its CP, MOp, MOs, ORBl, and SSp recordings show significant modality
generalization conditional on that session; its CP recording also shows a
positive baseline–modality association (correlation 0.309, q = 0.00249).
These observations cannot establish a naive-population trend or an effect of
learning. None of the five added areas is represented in this annotated naive
session. The session audit lists the additional naive recordings missing anatomy.

## 5. Spatial organization

For early modality preference, within-probe spatial covariance exceeds its
shuffle expectation at q < .05 in ACAv, CA1, CP, MOp, MOs, ORBvl, SCm, SSp, SSs,
and VISp. The mouse bootstrap interval for the excess over the null is also
positive in CP, MOs, ORBvl, SCm, SSp, SSs, and VISp. For baseline context
preference, CP and MOs satisfy both criteria; SCm passes the conditional shuffle
test (q = 0.0162), but its across-mouse excess interval includes zero.

A useful individual-session example is **SCm, 714753_2024-07-01**: 71 cells at
37 recording sites show within-probe organization of both early modality
preference and baseline context preference (session-family q = 0.00243 for each).
All supported sessions, including nonsignificant examples, appear in the PDFs.

Spatial effects should be compared with their permutation expectation, which
can be negative in small, centered samples. A negative raw covariance is not
automatically evidence against clustering. Coordinates estimate recording sites,
and pooled SCm organization can reflect differences among its subdivisions.
Null results do not demonstrate spatial mixing at finer scales than the data resolve.
